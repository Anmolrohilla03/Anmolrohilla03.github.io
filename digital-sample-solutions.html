Smart Asset Tracking Ecosystem for Hospitals

A connected RFID-based system designed for tablets, handhelds, and dashboards

The Problem

The Everyday Chaos

Picture a nurse walking into a ward, needing an infusion pump immediately. She checks one room—it's gone. Another—also empty. By the time she finds a working unit, ten minutes have passed. Multiply that across teams and devices and you see the hidden cost: wasted time, delayed care, and frustration.

The Invisible Trail

Behind every device is a paper log or spreadsheet that's rarely up to date. Assets move without being recorded, and soon no one knows which ventilator is clean, which is in repair, and which is simply missing. Manual steps don't scale.

The Challenge

Hospitals already have tablets, Wi-Fi, and RFID tags. The gap was a unified experience that connects them. We needed a system that logs movement automatically, validates sanitization, and updates every team in real time—without changing how staff work.

Mission: Turn every room, device, and interaction into one connected story—where equipment never goes missing, and care never has to wait.

The Solution

To solve the challenges I uncovered, I designed SmartTrack — a connected ecosystem that automatically tracks, validates, and locates hospital equipment in real time. My goal was to create a simple yet powerful experience that helps hospital staff save time, stay compliant, and maintain complete visibility of every asset.

Tablet – Room-Level Tracking

I designed the tablet interface to be used in every room for checking assets in and out and logging sanitization status. Each action is RFID-enabled — allowing staff to complete tasks with a quick scan instead of manual typing.

Cube Reader – Entry/Exit Validation

I defined how cube readers work at hallway entry and exit points to automatically record when an asset moves between floors. This eliminated the need for manual logging and reduced tracking errors caused by missed handoffs.

Handheld Device – Asset Locator

I designed the handheld experience to help staff locate misplaced or critical assets using RFID scanning. It displays proximity strength and direction, allowing users to pinpoint equipment within seconds anywhere in the hospital.

TV Dashboard – Real-Time Visibility

I created a TV dashboard for admin teams to monitor assets across all floors. It provides quick visibility into which equipment is available, in use, in repair, or delayed — helping teams make faster, data-driven decisions.

Designing for Every Touchpoint

Each SmartTrack module was designed with its own workflow and user context in mind. I explored what I already knew, what I learned through research, and how those insights shaped my wireframes and final designs for every touchpoint — tablet, cube, handheld, and dashboard.

🧠

What I Knew

I observed that staff were manually logging assets using paper checklists across multiple rooms. This process was slow, error-prone, and often skipped when workloads were high.

Each time an infusion pump, monitor, or wheelchair moved between areas, someone had to write down its ID, location, and time — which meant lost records were common. Manual tracking also made it difficult to identify which equipment was available, in use, or missing, leading to unnecessary delays and reordering.

🔍

What I Researched

Through observation, I noticed that staff spent unnecessary time switching between multiple screens or forms to record each asset. When nurses were busy, they often skipped steps — not because of neglect, but because the process demanded too much cognitive effort during peak hours.

I mapped their workflow across clean, soiled, and in-use rooms to understand when and why tracking dropped off. This revealed an opportunity to design a single-screen, guided scanning flow that could reduce the number of taps and simplify the entire check-in/out process without disrupting their routine.
