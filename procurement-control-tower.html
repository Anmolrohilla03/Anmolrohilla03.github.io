Designing a Unified Control Tower to Empower Procurement Teams with Data-Driven Decision Making

Procurement teams relied on disconnected dashboards, spreadsheets, supplier portals, emails, and reports to monitor operations and make purchasing decisions. Switching between multiple systems created fragmented workflows, delayed insights, and made strategic decision-making reactive rather than proactive.

My goal was to design a unified Procurement Control Tower that centralized operational data, AI-assisted insights, communication, and daily workflows into a single experience—helping procurement teams make faster, more informed decisions with confidence.

What Wasn't Working and What I Set Out to Solve

Challenge

  • 🧩 Scattered tools across teams (emails, spreadsheets, legacy dashboards)
  • ⏳ Manual delays in reporting & task tracking
  • 🔄 Miscommunication across departments
  • 👁️ Limited visibility for leadership
  • 📂 Training resources hard to access

Goal

  • 🧭 Centralize dashboards, tasks, training, and documentation
  • ⚡ Streamline workflows across procurement teams
  • 🧠 Improve decision-making through visibility
  • 📈 Build a scalable, intuitive, and efficient platform
  • 🔄 Enable faster onboarding and cross-functional adoption
These challenges revealed that the core issue was not a lack of information but disconnected workflows, fragmented tools, and limited operational visibility. This shaped my design strategy to create a centralized workspace that unified decision making, collaboration, and operational insights, enabling procurement teams to work more efficiently and make informed decisions.

Discover & Research

Insights gathered from procurement professionals guided every design decision.

Before exploring solutions, I wanted to understand how procurement professionals managed their daily workflows, where existing processes created friction, and what information they needed to make confident purchasing decisions. Through stakeholder interviews, user surveys, and workflow analysis, I uncovered usability gaps, trust concerns, and operational inefficiencies that directly shaped the design strategy.

Which areas of the dashboard do you rely on most?
"The KPI card gives me a pulse check every morning. I wish I could rearrange or hide the ones not relevant to my role."
→ Add customizable KPI modules.
Do you find any part of the dashboard confusing or hard to locate?
"I wasn't sure at first where the RFP templates or pipeline analysis were. The links are there, but the titles could be more action-oriented."
→ Use contextual link labels like 'Open Template' and add icons.
How does the workflow/task area help your daily work?
"It's great to see tasks and systems linked — but a filter to see 'Overdue Only' would save me clicks."
→ Add task filters and sorting features.
Has this dashboard improved your team collaboration?
"The Yammer updates and reminders section are super helpful. It'd be even better if I could @tag someone in reminders."
→ Enable @mentions in notes or reminders.

Key Research Insights

  • Procurement teams relied on multiple disconnected systems.
  • Users struggled to quickly locate critical procurement information.
  • Collaboration features lacked visibility and context.
  • Teams wanted clearer navigation and more proactive guidance.

Final Product Experience

Guided by research insights, I designed a unified procurement platform that centralized workflows, operational visibility, collaboration, onboarding, and embedded support into a single experience. Every feature was intentionally designed to reduce context switching and help procurement teams complete tasks more efficiently.

Unified Procurement Dashboard

Unified Workflow Management

Procurement teams previously relied on multiple disconnected systems to manage tasks, deadlines, and operational activities. The dashboard brings reminders, workflows, and priorities together into one centralized workspace so users can quickly understand what requires attention.

Operational Visibility

Key procurement metrics, workflow status, and business KPIs are surfaced together to provide immediate visibility into ongoing operations. Users can monitor progress, identify risks, and make informed decisions without navigating multiple reports.

Centralized Resource Hub

Frequently used procurement documents, templates, reports, and shared resources are available directly within the dashboard, reducing search time and supporting faster execution of everyday work.

Embedded Collaboration

Announcements, discussions, and team communication are integrated directly into the dashboard, allowing procurement teams to collaborate within the context of their work instead of switching between separate applications.

Guided Onboarding

Research showed that first-time users often struggled to understand where to begin within the platform. To reduce the learning curve, I designed a contextual onboarding experience that gradually introduced key workflows instead of relying on traditional training.
01

Interactive Walkthroughs

Step-by-step guidance introduces users to important features during their first experience without interrupting their workflow.

02

Contextual Guidance

Context-sensitive tooltips explain unfamiliar functionality as users encounter different parts of the platform, making learning feel natural.

03

Progressive Adoption

Users can move through onboarding at their own pace using guided steps, progress indicators, and dismissible tours, improving confidence while reducing onboarding time.

Integrated Support Center

Instead of requiring users to leave the product for documentation, I designed an embedded support center that provides learning resources, FAQs, and troubleshooting directly within the platform.
01

Searchable Knowledge Base

Training materials, documentation, and operational resources are organized in one searchable location for quick access.

02

Self-Service Support

Frequently asked questions help users resolve common issues independently, reducing dependency on support teams.

03

Embedded Help Resources

Integrated guidance and support links allow users to find answers without leaving their current workflow, creating a smoother overall experience.

Key Design Tradeoffs

The final experience was shaped by several tradeoffs between usability, stakeholder priorities, and product complexity.

Information Density vs. Clarity

Challenge

Stakeholders wanted more metrics, shortcuts, and resources visible on the dashboard.

Decision

Prioritized high-value information and placed secondary content behind progressive disclosure.

Impact

A cleaner dashboard that made important information easier to scan.

AI Assistance vs. User Trust

Challenge

AI insights needed to provide value without overwhelming users or appearing to replace human judgment.

Decision

Embedded concise insight cards into the existing workflow and clearly labeled AI-generated content.

Impact

Relevant intelligence remained visible while preserving transparency and user control.

Guidance vs. Workflow Efficiency

Challenge

First-time users needed support, while experienced users wanted immediate access to their work.

Decision

Used contextual, dismissible onboarding instead of a mandatory tutorial.

Impact

New users received guidance without slowing down experienced procurement teams.

Key Learnings

Designing this procurement platform reinforced that great enterprise experiences aren't about presenting more information—they're about helping users make better decisions with the right information at the right time. Working closely with stakeholders throughout the project also strengthened my ability to balance business priorities, user needs, and technical constraints.

Prioritize Decisions, Not Data

Enterprise dashboards become more effective when information is organized around user decisions instead of simply displaying available data.

Collaboration Drives Better Outcomes

Continuous collaboration with product managers, engineers, and stakeholders helped refine priorities early and reduce costly design changes later.

Simplicity Scales

Establishing consistent interaction patterns made the platform easier to learn, easier to maintain, and better prepared for future enhancements.

Systems Thinking

Looking beyond individual screens helped create connected workflows that felt cohesive across the entire procurement experience.

These lessons continue to shape how I approach enterprise product design—balancing user needs, business goals, and scalable system thinking to create intuitive experiences for complex workflows.
